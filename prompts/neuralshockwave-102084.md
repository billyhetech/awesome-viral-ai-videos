## Prompt

```text
SEEDANCE 2.5 — LOS ANGELES HELICOPTER MOTORCYCLE PURSUIT

Duration: 30 seconds
Aspect ratio: 16:9 widescreen
Frame rate: 24 fps
Visual style: Ultra-realistic viral helicopter pursuit footage, raw local-news / eyewitness recording, unpolished documentary realism.
CORE VISUAL CONCEPT
A chaotic, high-stakes police pursuit filmed entirely from a single helicopter-mounted aerial camera above Los Angeles in bright California daylight. The footage feels like an unexpected live-news incident captured by an experienced but struggling camera operator. The result must look like authentic broadcast footage, not a polished action movie.
Harsh midday sunlight, hard-edged shadows, heat shimmer rising from asphalt, dusty concrete, palm-lined streets, freeway interchanges, industrial blocks, parking lots, overpasses, and a wide concrete flood-control channel. Distant downtown Los Angeles appears through atmospheric haze.

Camera characteristics: Long-lens aerial footage, natural helicopter vibration, irregular handheld-style corrections, abrupt optical zooms, occasional focus hunting, imperfect framing, brief tracking errors, compression artifacts, realistic motion blur, and rapid but physically plausible pans. The camera must feel heavy and airborne, with delayed reactions when changing direction.

CHARACTER AND VEHICLE CONTINUITY

Main subject: The exact same motorcycle rider represented @image1 riding the exact same heavy motorcycle @image2.

Treat all supplied material references as visual references. Preserve the rider’s identity, clothing, body proportions, motorcycle silhouette, paint, colors, mechanical details, and overall scale. Do not redesign or replace either subject between shots.

The rider is highly skilled, aggressive, and controlled, leaning naturally through turns, absorbing landings with realistic suspension compression, and maintaining convincing balance at high speed.

Multiple police vehicles pursue him in the background. Maintain consistent vehicle appearance and believable road positions.

SHOT-BY-SHOT TIMELINE

00:00–00:05 — IMMEDIATE VIRAL HOOK

The helicopter camera is already searching frantically over a busy Los Angeles boulevard. Suddenly, the motorcycle rider blasts into frame between moving police cars. The camera operator snaps into a fast optical zoom, briefly overshoots, and corrects the framing.

The rider cuts sharply across the edge of a parking lot, jumps a low curb, and disappears momentarily behind palm trees before reappearing. A brief close aerial zoom catches the spinning rear wheel, visible tire rotation, and suspension movement as the motorcycle accelerates away.

Police sirens begin overlapping with the deep, throaty motorcycle engine. Strong helicopter rotor vibration is audible throughout.

00:05–00:10 — CHAOTIC PURSUIT

The rider rejoins the boulevard and threads through moving traffic, narrowly passing between vehicles before veering up a sloped driveway. He launches over a low concrete divider in one clean, controlled jump.

Several police cars follow on the roadway, their red-and-blue lights flashing in the harsh sunlight and their sirens echoing between buildings.

The helicopter camera over-zooms and briefly loses the motorcycle behind roadside structures. The operator rapidly pans back and reacquires the rider just as both wheels land. Show believable suspension compression, tire contact, and immediate forward acceleration.

00:10–00:15 — FLOOD CHANNEL REVEAL

The motorcycle enters a sloped service access and drops into a vast concrete flood-control channel. The helicopter camera pulls back into a wide aerial reveal, exposing the channel’s enormous scale, angled concrete banks, drainage seams, and distant bridges.

The rider accelerates along the channel floor, leaving a faint trail of dust and road grit. The helicopter banks to maintain visual contact, and the camera tilts steeply downward.

The operator then punches in toward the motorcycle’s rear wheel and suspension as the bike races along the concrete surface. Deep engine noise echoes naturally through the channel.

00:15–00:20 — WALL-RIDE STUNT

The rider suddenly steers up the angled concrete channel wall, climbing diagonally while maintaining realistic tire contact and body positioning. He holds a dramatic high line for a brief moment before descending smoothly toward the flat channel floor.

The helicopter banks hard to follow the maneuver. The long-lens camera shakes and briefly loses the rider near the edge of the frame before recovering him.

The motorcycle’s engine pitch rises under acceleration, then changes as the rider descends and regains the channel floor. Police radio chatter becomes more active, with clipped, overlapping transmissions from the pursuing units.

00:20–00:25 — UNEXPECTED ESCAPE ROUTE

The rider exits through a sloped service ramp and crosses beneath a broad concrete overpass. The helicopter camera continues tracking along the original trajectory, but the motorcycle unexpectedly emerges on a parallel industrial access road.

The camera operator reacts late, swings rapidly across the overpass, and catches the rider accelerating over a natural rise in the road. The motorcycle launches into a long, clean jump in full California daylight.

Capture the complete silhouette against the bright sky, with the rider maintaining a compact, stable posture. The bike follows a believable ballistic arc, with realistic forward momentum and gravity. The helicopter camera zooms in during the jump but struggles to keep the entire motorcycle inside the frame.

00:25–00:30 — ICONIC DISAPPEARANCE

The motorcycle lands on the industrial road with a firm suspension compression and continues accelerating toward another overpass. A quick zoom catches the spinning rear wheel as the rider rushes into the deep shadow underneath.

The helicopter camera overshoots the entrance, then circles and searches across the surrounding roads, concrete structures, and industrial blocks. The rider cannot be found.

Over the helicopter’s radio, an adult male reporter says in a stunned, restrained voice:

“We just lost him.”

The helicopter continues a shaky, wide aerial search over Los Angeles. Police sirens fade into the distance while the helicopter engine and rotor thump remain prominent.

Hard cut to black at exactly 30 seconds.

SOUND DESIGN — CRITICAL

Create a continuous, layered, realistic location-audio mix. Avoid artificial action-movie sound effects.

Motorcycle: Deep, throaty, realistic heavy-motorcycle engine with changing RPM, aggressive acceleration, throttle blips, gear changes, and Doppler shifts as the rider moves relative to the helicopter.

Police vehicles: Authentic emergency-vehicle sirens, shifting in volume and pitch with distance, mixed with faint tire noise and engine sounds.

Helicopter: Constant rotor thump, turbine whine, airframe vibration, and subtle wind buffeting the onboard microphone.

Police radio: Realistic, narrow-band radio transmissions with static, brief squelch bursts, clipped speech, overlapping dispatch and pursuing-unit communication, and occasional unintelligible background chatter. The voices should sound as though they originate inside the helicopter or through its radio equipment.
Environment: Los Angeles traffic ambience, distant horns, freeway noise, and natural echoes inside the concrete flood channel.
Reporter: One brief, clear male spoken line at the end: “We just lost him.” No narration over the stunts.
Maintain a believable audio perspective. The motorcycle engine should become louder as the rider approaches the camera’s tracking area and quieter as he disappears into the distance. Do not let the engine sound unnaturally close during wide aerial shots.
REALISM AND MOTION CONSTRAINTS
One continuous helicopter-based observational perspective, with no ground-level camera cuts, drone shots, third-person chase angles, or impossible camera teleportation. Natural reframing and optical zoom changes are allowed.

Maintain consistent rider and motorcycle identity across all 30 seconds. All stunts must remain readable, with physically plausible motorcycle traction, suspension travel, acceleration, takeoff, airtime, and landing.

The helicopter must follow plausible aerial trajectories and cannot instantly change position. Maintain recognizable Los Angeles urban geography and consistent daylight direction.

Negative prompt: Cinematic color grading, glossy blockbuster visuals, artificial slow motion, impossible motorcycle physics, floating wheels, distorted motorcycle geometry, duplicate riders, changing outfits, changing bike design, warped police vehicles, teleportation, excessive motion blur, exaggerated explosions, crashes, gore, readable text, watermarks, visible brand logos, and artificial or repetitive sound effects.
```
